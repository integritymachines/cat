Headline contribution: allocation cannot be observed, and its two traces can. The paper states tests of presence and of degree for single instances, the cases the account must survive, and the outcomes that would refute it.

## Abstract (0.25 page)

- Claim: the account is tested at the instance. Presence asks whether an instance receives allocation. Degree asks how much.
- Claim: presence depends on use and consequence at fixed input. No account that ties presence to the input alone predicts this.
- Shape of support: an observation model, estimators, eleven tests, four dissociations, one refutation condition.

## 1. Introduction (1.5 pages)

- Context: the field has moved from correlates to adversarial tests of theories. Most measures address the state of the whole brain or a stage of processing.
- Contribution: tests posed for one instance, with the operators of Paper 3 as the quantities to estimate.
- Novelty: presence and degree are separated. Marks usually read as signs of consciousness (ignition, braking, frontal theta) are assigned to one regime.
- Stance on evidence: the paper reports what existing studies show and marks its own predictions as predictions. It states no effect size it has not sourced.
- Depends on: Papers 1 and 3.

## 2. What Is Measured (2 pages)

- Claim: allocation is a quantity per instance. It is not observed.
- Claim: two traces are observed. Metabolic expenditure at the instance, and control work with its effect on the trajectory.
- Claim: the ratio of the two is an efficiency. It separates effort from output.
- Claim: an outside observer measures traces and not experience. The paper takes this as a premise and does not prove it.
- Support: where state-level measures sit. Perturbational complexity tracks whether anything can receive allocation. It says nothing about which instance does.
- Kernel: §4 opening, Assumptions 4.1 and 4.2.
- Depends on: Section 1.

## 3. Estimators (2 pages)

- Claim: each operator of Paper 3 needs an estimator from recorded data. The map Laplacian from connectivity. Conductance from its pseudoinverse. Use from task flow. Divergence from the mismatch of predicted and encountered input. Tolerance from the onset of control.
- Claim: each estimator has a validity condition. State it with the estimator.
- Status: all [open]. This section is where the paper is weakest and should say so.
- Kernel: estimators, §5 last column.
- Depends on: Section 2 and Paper 3.

## 4. Presence (2.5 pages)

- Test 4.3, contrast. Same input, reported against unreported. Evidence to review: no-report paradigms, the posterior negativity against the late positivity.
- Test 4.4, fading. Stable predicted input of low use. Evidence to review: perceptual fading, habituation.
- Test 4.5, relevance. Detection of a change rises with use and consequence at fixed physical change. Evidence to review: change blindness, inattentional blindness.
- Test 4.6, global loss. Under anesthesia and dreamless sleep, expenditure at any instance stops depending on divergence and use. Evidence to review: propofol against ketamine.
- Claim: Test 4.5 is the distinctive one.
- Kernel: Tests 4.3 to 4.6.
- Depends on: Sections 2 and 3.

## 5. Degree (2.5 pages)

- Test 4.7, monitoring. Before any divergence, expenditure rises with use times consequence.
- Test 4.8, the critical level. Control begins where conductance falls below divergence over tolerance. With no divergence, no bottleneck triggers it.
- Test 4.9, slowing. Response time near the transition rises with consequence.
- Test 4.10, regime cost. At matched warrant, expenditure is larger when no supported alternative exists.
- Test 4.11, data limits. Degrading the input lowers the evidence for a divergence and does not raise the cost of repair. Evidence to review: the multiple-demand network is flat to motion coherence and scales with rule complexity.
- Claim: Tests 4.8 and 4.10 follow from Paper 3 alone and from no stand-in.
- Kernel: Tests 4.7 to 4.11.
- Depends on: Sections 2 and 3.

## 6. Dissociations (1.5 pages)

- Experience without behavior: ketamine, dreaming. Allocation on internal instances, actuators uncoupled.
- Behavior without experience: automatisms. No allocation on the instances that drive the behavior.
- Braking without awareness: masked stop signals. The brake marks conflict and not presence.
- Performance without strain: flow. High efficiency.
- Claim: each case is a prediction and each can fail.
- Kernel: dissociation table.
- Depends on: Sections 4 and 5.
